What the schema decides for you

  • The emoji go. Six of them along the bottom is the least editorial thing in the app. Words, letterspaced, no icons — and the bar gets shorter, not taller.
  • Pills become underlines. A filled black pill is a heavy object; the schema has exactly one filled field per composition and it's the olive footer.
  • The title gets its own line and stops competing with the back arrow and the sparkle. It's the name of a journey — it can have the room.
  • The day strip loses its chips. Numerals in the serif with a single-letter weekday above; the active day is the only olive on screen.

What it costs

  • "Useful" became "More", because six words don't fit where six icons did. That is a real loss of signposting, not a tidy-up.
  • Without icons the bar is slower to hit at a glance — you read it rather than recognising it. Fine on a page you visit; worse on one you use daily.
  • The day numerals are lovely and harder to tap. Chips were chips for a reason.

What this costs you

Every screen above is beautiful and three of them are less useful than what you have. That is the trade, and it is worth naming precisely rather than discovering later.

Takes it well

  • The journal. Long prose in a serif on paper is simply better, and the entry is the whole point of that screen.
  • Trip covers and the chapter cards — they already carry one photo and a date range.
  • The share page and the public site, which are read by people who don't need to operate anything.

Fights it

  • The trip screen above shows "four still to sort". Today it shows you which four nights, in which city, with the evidence and two buttons. That is the feature.
  • The trips list drops the covers, the flags and the leg counts. Pretty, and now you can't tell Bangkok 2026 from a Bangkok you haven't taken yet.
  • The review queue isn't here at all, because it can't be — the contradiction, the flight and five answers have to share one screen or the reasoning disappears.
  • The flights log is 517 rows. This schema has no answer for row 400.

So: half the app

Narrative surfaces take the whole schema — paper, olive, serif, three things per screen. Working surfaces keep the density they have and borrow only the palette, so the two halves still look like one app. That split is real and it already exists in the code: the tabs you read versus the tabs you operate.
