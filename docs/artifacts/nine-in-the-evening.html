Nine in the evening

Three directions for the screen, and the question it ends on. Everything shown here is computed from a real day — Rome, 23 January: 117 photographs between 06:06 and 20:14, 53 of them buildings, 15 of them animals, one piece of art.

First, the part that is already built

Fourteen nights, fourteen different notifications

The variety isn't a phrase bank behind a random number — randomness repeats itself about a third of the time. It comes from angles: several genuinely different things that are all true of the same day. Which one leads is free.

Quirk comes from arithmetic and juxtaposition, never invention. “53 buildings. And 1 piece of art.” is funny because it’s true. “Rome has opinions about cats” is funnier, and is a thing this app does not know — the night it says one of those is the night nobody believes the numbers either.

The three directions

What opens when you tap it

Same data, same question at the end. Entirely different feeling. The lock screen is identical in all three, so it’s only the tap that changes.

A — The Reel

Your own photographs, full bleed, one every three seconds, numbers counting up over them. Eight seconds and it’s done.

Biggest wow · needs photographs · least re-watchable

B — The Postcard

One still composition that holds together as an image. The only one of the three you would screenshot and send to someone.

Shareable · quieter · works with six photographs or none

C — The Page

A page of the diary that wrote itself. Closest to what the app already is, and the only one where the writing does the work.

Truest to the app · lowest wow · ages best over fourteen nights

My read:

A on the first night, C on the fourteenth. The reel is the wow and it wears out; the page is the one you’d still open on day twelve. That points at B as the shape, with A’s motion spent only on a day that earns it — a first visit, a flight, a personal best. The rest of the time it settles.

The question at the bottom

This is the part that pays for the feature

The story pipeline asks its questions days or weeks later — who were you with at the Trevi? — by which point the honest answer is that nobody remembers. Asked the same evening, the answer is real. And a real answer is testimony, the top rung of the evidence ladder, which the reconstruction is otherwise forced to guess at.

Who was with you?

Two taps. Feeds every future sentence about that day, and the app currently infers it from faces or not at all.

Asked once per trip, not nightly

Was this the day you…?

Confirms a deduced leg. “Heathrow to Fiumicino, the 16:15?” — turning a likely into a confirmed.

Only when deduction is unsure between two

What was that place?

One photograph the seeing pass couldn’t name, at a coordinate. You’ll know. In a fortnight you won’t.

Max one a night, skippable, never twice

  • One question a night. Never two. The recap is a gift; the question is the price, and it has to stay small enough not to feel like one.
  • Skipping is a valid answer and must never be asked again that trip.
  • No question on a quiet day. If there’s nothing to celebrate there’s nothing to trade for.
