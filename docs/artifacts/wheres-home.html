Route B · my recommendation

Lead with it

Your better idea, and it is better for a reason worth naming.

It's the only question the app can't work out on its own. Dates, places and durations all come from the photos. Home doesn't — and without it "away" has no meaning, so B4's sentence is impossible in Route A.

It also gives the empty globe something to hold on day one, which is the screen you were looking at when you asked where the demos went.

On the wording: "Is your pond the UK?" is charming and slightly unclear. Lead with the plain question and spend the charm on the answer — B2 asks Where's home?, B3 replies Your pond is the UK. Clear going in, characterful coming out.

What the phone can tell us without asking

Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone returns Europe/London with no permission prompt, no dialog, no GPS. So the three flags aren't a guess — we put the right one at the top and pre-tick it, and the question becomes a confirmation. That's the frictionless version of asking, and it's why B2 shows one already ticked.

"No, just photos" — what actually happens

We keep them. Binning a person's photos because they declined a suggestion would be indefensible, and it teaches them never to tap No again. They go into Photos as loose photos with a permanent one-tap route into a trip.

And we ask once more, at most. If the Canada cluster grows by another three days it's worth a second offer. After that it stays quiet forever. Two offers is helpful, three is nagging.

I'd also relabel it — "No, keep them loose" says what happens. "Just photos" sounds like they're being thrown away.

Skip for now → no tooltips

You asked whether Skip lands on tooltips. It shouldn't. The card from Four already says whose trips those are, in one line, with nothing to dismiss — which is the entire job the tour was invented for. That makes the coach-mark rebuild a nice-to-have rather than a blocker.

The order of asking

Home → photos → is this a trip → location. Email and sign-in are not in this list. They buy nothing on day one; they matter the day someone gets a second device, and that is when to ask.

For the trip they've already booked, the way in goes above the ideas, not below. Someone with something booked isn't browsing, and forwarding a confirmation is the single highest-value thing they can do. It's also machinery you already have.
