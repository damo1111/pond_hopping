The asset — corrected

Not an empty room. But a smaller one than this page first claimed.

Nine arcs — and the plan is to cut that to one trip. Rome only. China & Japan is a second retro example that adds nothing: five flights and no photographs, so tapping it opens a trip with nothing in it, which is worse than it not being there.

Which undoes the argument this page was built on. With Rome alone a new hopper's globe carries four arcs — Edinburgh to Heathrow, Heathrow to Fiumicino, and back — all of them clustered over western Europe. A globe is a spectacular hero for an account with 486 flights across seventeen years. It is a weak one for somebody on day one looking at four short-haul arcs.

The revision

The globe is the wrong first screen. Rome is.

Not a tour about the app — the app having already finished, on real content, that you can scroll: 103 photographs, a written story, four flights, a day-by-day trace. “This is what a trip looks like once this app has been at it.” That is the whole pitch and it needs no cards, no tooltips and no carousel.

The globe is the container. Rome is the demonstration. Direction A survives with its subject swapped.

And it promotes something already on the backlog. “Make demo should duplicate, not convert” is why there are so few: the switch turns a real trip into the example rather than copying it, so each one is built by hand, which is exactly why Rome was. That stops being housekeeping and becomes the thing the door stands on.

Still open: Lisbon & Porto has China & Japan's defect — no flights, no photographs, an empty draft. It exists to show a trip that has not happened yet, which is a genuinely different state and worth demonstrating. So either fill it properly or drop it and demonstrate the retro case alone. As it stands it demonstrates nothing.

The rest of the case below is untouched by any of this: four mechanisms are still three abandoned patterns and a form, and four uncoordinated memories are still four.

What the field settled on

The 2026 consensus is narrower than it used to be

Users pick up their phones during fragmented attention windows and have a much lower tolerance for being walked through anything. The top performers share a small set of patterns: a meaningful first action, progressive disclosure, and an empty state that feels filled. Userpilot · Plotline · Appcues, 2026
  • Do-first beats tell-first. One activation moment, not a product tour. The small investment of doing a thing creates ownership; watching a carousel creates nothing.
  • Contextual, at the moment of need. A hint where the action is possible reads and retains far better than the same hint on a wall at launch.
  • The empty state is the tutorial — Slack teaches Threads in the space where threads will be.
  • Ask for context later. Value first, then the form. Every field before the value is a chance to leave.

Read against what we have: IntroCards is the carousel, DemoTour is the product tour, and Onboarding is the form-before-value. Three of the four mechanisms are the patterns the field has spent five years abandoning.

Three ways it could go

Directions

A — Open on Rome

First open is the example trip itself, already written, already full of photographs. No cards, no tour. One persistent control: Tip it in. The globe is behind it, not in front of it.

Teaching happens only where an action is possible, once, and never again.

Boldest · deletes ~1,200 lines · needs the demo trips to be good first

B — One question, then work

A single screen: What have you got? — photographs, a Google Timeline export, an email to forward, or nothing yet. Then straight into that path.

Keeps a deliberate front door but reduces it from six steps to one choice.

Safest · keeps the four ways in · still a gate before the room

C — Earn it

Nothing at first open. The app teaches itself as things arrive: the first story finishing, the first flight enriching, the first trip built. Each moment gets one celebration and one next step.

The animation budget goes on outcomes, never on instructions.

Best long tail · slowest to land the first “oh” · most to build

Where I would go

A for the door, C for everything after it

Open on the globe with nothing in the way, and spend the entire wow budget on the moment the first real story comes back — because that is the app's actual promise, and no card at launch can make that promise as well as the thing itself keeping it.

B's question survives, but moved: it belongs on the empty state of Tip it in, not in front of the globe. Which is the same instinct as the recap — the gift first, the ask second.

Before anything gets built

Five things I would want decided

  • Does the demo trip stay explorable signed out? Direction A depends on it entirely. Today a signed-out tap gets “this trip isn't yours to edit” — a door in the face at exactly the moment we want a door open. That's backlog item 5 and it becomes load-bearing.
  • Is the cold open kept? The globe flying in is genuinely good and it is also a thing between the person and the app. Keep it once, ever — or keep it every launch?
  • What happens to the partner email? It's the only real question in the current form. Moved to the moment it matters — the first trip with two people on it — or dropped?
  • How much can be deleted? A is ~1,200 lines removed. That is the good kind of change and it is still a deletion of working code with no way back but git.
  • What is the one activation moment? My answer: a story coming back. Not a trip created, not photographs uploaded — the moment it hands you writing about your own life that you didn't write. Everything else is setup for that.
