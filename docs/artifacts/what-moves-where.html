Being straight about it

Two things genuinely are lost

The guarantee. A carousel is the only pattern that promises every single person sees the pitch, in order, before anything else. Contextual copy is read by more of the people who reach it and by none of the people who never do.

The twenty-second visitor. Somebody who opens the app, looks, and leaves without tapping anything currently learns what it does. Under the proposal they learn nothing at all — they just see a stranger’s trip to Rome and go.

That second one is the real argument against, and it is not small. It is also the argument the carousel was invented to win, which is why every app has one and why the research keeps saying they do not work: the twenty-second visitor was probably never going to stay, and the cost is charged to everybody else.

Revised, because of the above

Keep one card, not none

The change to my own proposal

Deleting all three cards loses the twenty-second visitor for nothing. Deleting two loses almost none of it. Keep card one — “Tip it in” — as a single panel over the example trip, dismissible, once ever. It is the only card that says what the app is for; the other two say what it will look like and how privacy works, and both of those are answers to questions nobody has yet.

So: one card, not a carousel of three. No tour. The tour’s only irreplaceable line moves onto the trip it is about. And the form goes.

  • Still deleted: the three-step tour overlay with its anchor-resolving and re-measuring, cards two and three, and the onboarding form as a gate. Around 900 lines rather than 1,200.
  • Still one arbiter instead of four uncoordinated “seen it” memories. That part was never about copy and is the fix that actually prevents the bad first run.
  • Still the celebration moves to the first story coming back, which no card can do and which is the only moment that proves the promise instead of making it.
